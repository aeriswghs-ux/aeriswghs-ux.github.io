When the smoke system became the problem.

An archived build account of setbacks, a suggestion from Rithika, and the change to dry-ice visualization.

Research objective

Make the tunnel flow visible while developing the measurement system.

Background

The original smoke approach led to repeated changes in materials, filaments, air pumps, and the overall setup. This entry preserves the October 3 build account rather than reconstructing an unrecorded sequence of experiments.

The design decision

Rithika proposed switching to dry ice. Rithwik reported that the resulting visualization worked smoothly. In the October 13 clarification, the owner described dry ice in water in front of the inlet, air blown into the setup, and an outlet fan drawing air through the tunnel.

Observations

The reported improvement concerns visible flow and practical operation. It does not establish uniform velocity, calibrated airspeed, lift, or drag. No run-by-run photographs or measurements were supplied.

What went wrong

The smoke system became a recurring source of setup changes. The available record identifies the types of changes but does not include a dated failure log, costs, or comparisons under controlled conditions.

What the iteration shows

The practical response was to change the visualization method. The next challenge is to make the force-measurement stand equally inspectable through design files, calibration, and test records.

Next steps

Add original photographs of the setup and a dated description of the final arrangement when they are available. Keep qualitative observations separate from quantitative results.
