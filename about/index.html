Start with something you can question.

Our current work follows a wind tunnel from its physical build toward a more useful measurement system. Along the way are print files, sensor code, analysis tools, and a visualization setup that took a different direction after repeated setbacks.

The fieldbook makes that process visible: an idea, a build, a problem, a revision, and the next question.

Rithwik Krishna Gummadi

Main builder & researcher

Rithwik leads the wind-tunnel build and experimental research. The current focus is the measurement stand and a planned 3D model to accommodate airfoils and model aircraft.

Rithika Priya Gummadi

Documentation, iteration & CubeSat exploration

Rithika documents changes and observations, proposed the dry-ice visualization approach, helped review code, and researches CubeSat possibilities.

Learning across the boundaries.

Aerodynamics leads into sensors. Sensors lead into code. Code leads back to the question of what a number means. The projects connect aerospace engineering with fabrication, scientific computing, and the habit of checking assumptions.

Formal coursework and additional academic details have not been added to this record. The linked work shows the independent learning currently documented.

Make room for the unfinished.

A smooth-looking plot does not establish accuracy. An illustration does not establish a design. This fieldbook keeps source artifacts, reported observations, and verified results distinct, so the next person can understand both the work and its limits.

Where the questions lead next.

Finish the sensor stand, document the mounting model, complete calibration, and publish repeatable observations. In parallel, explore which questions might make a useful CubeSat research direction.
