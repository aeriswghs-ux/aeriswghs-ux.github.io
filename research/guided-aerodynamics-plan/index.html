Abstract

A stage-gated plan for turning an assembled low-cost wind tunnel into a characterized low-Reynolds-number instrument, then testing whether dynamic pitch offers a defensible original question. Revised 6 November 2026 after four researcher conversations. No experimental results are claimed.

Research question

Can a low-cost, characterized wind-tunnel system reliably quantify aerodynamic-force changes at low Reynolds number — and what changes when angle of attack moves dynamically rather than holding fixed?

Methodology

Advance only when the evidence for the current stage exists: finish and document the instrument; map the empty tunnel; reproduce a fixed-angle NACA 0018 baseline; choose one literature-defined question; cross-check the physics; publish the full record. The sequence is a research design, not a schedule.

Findings

No measured findings. The conversations changed the plan in three ways: validation comes before novelty, instrument characterization comes before force interpretation, and a dynamic-pitch experiment must pass a mechanical and temporal-response gate before it is treated as feasible. The estimated 50,000–100,000 Reynolds-number range and the verbal “8 by 8” test-section size remain unverified.

Limitations

Interview guidance is not experimental validation. No velocity map, calibration record, force dataset, measured Reynolds number, or confirmed literature gap exists yet. The dynamic-pitch direction, publication path, and mentoring arrangement are not guaranteed. Public attribution of the interviewees still requires confirmation.

Why the plan changed

The original objective was to build an affordable wind tunnel and investigate a conventional airfoil, beginning with NACA 0018. The facility now exists in some form, but the measurement system is unfinished. The interviews made that distinction central.

A fabricated tunnel is an engineering project. A validated instrument is a stronger claim: it produces a measured flow, a documented calibration, and a result another person can inspect. Repeating a well-studied NACA result may establish the second. It does not, by itself, establish a novel contribution.

The revised order is therefore: characterize, validate, then choose. The dynamic-angle question remains the preferred exploratory candidate, but only if the apparatus can resolve it without confusing aerodynamics with vibration, sensor lag, or mounting response.

Stage gates and exit evidence

Stage Objective Exit evidence
01 — Finish the instrument Turn the assembled tunnel into a documented measurement system: dimensions, rigid airfoil holder, repeatable angle stand, independent lift and drag channels, calibration references, and a raw-data format. Dimensioned diagram, wiring schematic, calibration log, data-logging demonstration, and an angle-setting repeatability check.
02 — Characterize the empty tunnel Map speed across the test section, quantify variation over time and between runs, and fit control input to measured airspeed rather than trusting fan specifications. Velocity map, centerline profile, control-to-speed fit with residuals, repeatability plot, and a written uncertainty statement.
03 — Reproduce the NACA 0018 baseline Compare fixed-angle lift and drag with published data at the closest achievable Reynolds number, aspect ratio, surface condition, and test geometry. Coefficient curves with uncertainty, traceable raw data and calculations, and a reasoned account of agreement or disagreement.
04 — Choose one original question Use a focused literature review and a feasibility matrix to select one question the apparatus can distinguish from noise, vibration, mounting artifacts, and flow non-uniformity. A written, cited gap; a comparable-conditions table; and an experimental design that can resolve the expected signal.
05 — Cross-check the physics Use CFD or a simpler aerodynamic model to test whether observed trends are plausible after the static baseline is understood — not to certify an uncalibrated instrument. Documented geometry and boundary conditions, mesh and convergence checks, and a clear account of where the model departs from the experiment.
06 — Publish the record Release the protocol, calibration, raw and cleaned data, analysis, null results, and limitations for inspection before pursuing a paper or competition. A reproducible report and external review. Publication is considered only when the evidence and contribution justify it.

No stage is complete because time has passed or a part has been built. It is complete when its exit evidence can be inspected. The first two stages are the current work; the later stages remain conditional.

Candidate original experiments

Direction Motivation from the interviews Feasibility or novelty condition
Transient angle of attack Compare the force during motion with the force at the same angle held steady; vary the rate of change. Controlled actuation, synchronized force/angle/velocity sampling, known sensor bandwidth, and a vibration test that can separate aerodynamic response from mount resonance.
Finite-span / three-dimensional effects Study how a real finite wing departs from the two-dimensional assumption behind a NACA section. Controlled aspect ratio, documented end conditions and wall effects, and a baseline that already agrees with section data.
Morphing or moving geometry Examine how a changing surface affects forces over time. A repeatable mechanism and enough independent measurements to separate geometry, motion rate, and mounting effects. Novelty is not assumed.
Reverse-flow airfoil orientation Explore a condition motivated by advancing and retreating rotor-blade aerodynamics. A confirmed literature gap, controlled orientation, and a test geometry that represents the intended condition.
Measurement-method study Use the characterization itself to establish what affordable hardware can and cannot resolve. A specific methodological comparison, a trusted reference, and an uncertainty analysis strong enough to support a claim about method rather than a single result.

The current preference is transient angle of attack on a finite NACA 0018 wing. That preference is provisional. A static finite-span comparison or a measurement-method study may be more credible if the dynamic rig cannot separate the expected force change from mechanical response.

What would stop the plan

The plan should change if any of these conditions fails:

  • The empty tunnel cannot hold a repeatable speed, or its non-uniformity is too large to assign a defensible Reynolds number.
  • The force channels cannot separate lift from drag, or their resolution is too coarse for the loads the airfoil produces.
  • A dynamic rig cannot distinguish aerodynamic response from structural vibration, motor noise, or sensor lag.
  • The focused literature review finds no gap this apparatus can address.

The response is to improve the instrument or narrow the question, not to reinterpret noise as a result. Negative or inconclusive findings belong in the record when they are honestly measured.

Immediate next actions

  1. Measure the actual test-section geometry and produce a dimensioned diagram. Confirm the units behind the verbal “8 by 8” estimate.
  2. Finish the rigid airfoil holder and repeatable angle stand. Record commanded versus measured angle.
  3. Run the first empty-tunnel velocity map at several settings, including center, edges, repeats, and stabilization time.

Review boundary and next mentor meeting

The Caltech researcher agreed to serve as a PCIS mentor at approximately one hour per month, with scheduling flexibility. That is conceptual feedback, not sustained hands-on supervision, laboratory placement, authorship, or institutional sponsorship. Publication remains an aspiration rather than a promised deliverable. Public names, titles, and permission to attribute the conversations still need confirmation.

The next review should bring measured characterization evidence and three concrete questions:

  1. What minimum velocity-field measurements and uncertainty checks would make this tunnel credible for low-Reynolds-number force measurements?
  2. Which published NACA 0018 data are sufficiently similar to the measured operating conditions to serve as a reasonable validation baseline?
  3. Would a dynamic-pitch experiment be realistically distinguishable from mechanical vibration and sensor response in this apparatus, or should static finite-span comparisons come first?
